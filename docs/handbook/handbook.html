01 / Executive summaryCV-DOC-01

A website that works the way the business does.

Coolvane does not sell air conditioning by atmosphere. Every project is selected on load, installed to a drawing, and handed over with measurements. The website now follows the same discipline — and the team can maintain it without writing code.

The public site explains the practice across ten focused pages. Behind it, a single content document feeds every headline, service, case study and process gate. When the team edits a section and saves, the public pages follow within the publishing window. There is no deploy, no developer ticket and no second copy of the truth.

The same back office carries the commercial file: enquiries, customers, GST invoices, payments, expenses, payroll, reminders and the customer portal. It is one authenticated workspace rather than a collection of disconnected tools.

03 / Design languageCV-DS-02

Two registers. One system.

Foundry is the dark, engineered register: control-room surfaces, precise rules and cool light. Atelier is its daylight counterpart: warm paper, editorial serif and quiet borders. They are not two websites. They are one token layer, cast twice.

Tokens, not duplicates

Colour, type, spacing, radius and depth are semantic variables. A component is written once and resolves correctly in both themes — including fills, borders and schematic lines.

A typographic hierarchy

An editorial serif carries the argument. A neutral sans carries the detail. A mono face marks references, labels and measurements — the language of a commissioning sheet.

Drawn, not decorated

Blueprints, system maps and process diagrams use stronger line tokens so they remain legible on paper. Connectors carry information; decorative layers never carry meaning.

Light-theme correction, completed. Primary actions now always carry a filled surface; ink panels remain ink; schematic connectors and diagram rows use the strong line tokens; and decorative parallax layers sit behind the information rather than collapsing it. The result is parity between the two themes, not a light version of the dark one.

07 / Billing & customer serviceCV-PRT-01

The customer sees the same truth.

The back office and the customer portal are two views of one record. An invoice issued in admin appears in the portal addressed to that customer. A visit booked in the portal appears on the dispatch board. Neither side has to ask the other what is current.

Back office
01

GST invoices. Draft, split, part-pay and print an A4 tax invoice without leaving the ledger.

02

Collections. Outstanding, overdue and part-paid states are visible at a glance, with reminders queued from the same record.

03

Costs and crew. Expenses, team records and monthly payroll sit beside the revenue they support.

Customer portal
01

Invoices and balance. Totals, payments and open balance, addressed to the signed-in email.

02

Next visit. Service, date, window, engineer and status — before the customer has to call.

03

Book or rebook. A portal booking creates a dispatch-board visit and a lead for the works team.

ACCESS

Admin and portal are authenticated separately. Administrator credentials are issued privately and are deliberately not printed in this handbook. Customer portal accounts see only the invoices, visits and contact details addressed to their own email.

08 / Why it mattersCV-BEN-05

Why this earns its place.

A website is not a brochure on a shelf. It is the first specification a buyer reads, the file the team maintains, and the place a customer checks before calling. These are the practical returns of treating it as one system.

01
One source of truth

Copy, services, cases and process gates come from one versioned document. The site cannot disagree with the studio.

02
Publishing without a developer

The team edits the sentence, image or figure it owns. Saving publishes; browsers follow within seconds.

03
A brand that holds

Two themes, one token layer. New pages inherit the same type, colour, spacing and contrast rules.

04
Commercial control

Leads, invoices, collections, expenses and payroll are visible in the same workspace as the website.

05
Customer confidence

The portal answers the two questions that create phone calls: what do I owe, and when is my visit?

06
Institutional memory

Versioned content and a permanent admin record keep the handover file intact as people and projects change.

Without a managed system

  • Copy changes wait for a developer or a monthly batch.
  • The site and the sales deck drift into different claims.
  • Invoices and visits live in separate spreadsheets.
  • Customers call to ask what they already know.
  • Light and dark themes are maintained as two codebases.

With the Coolvane system

  • The team publishes the change it owns, the same day.
  • Every page reads from the same approved record.
  • Invoices, payments and visits share one ledger.
  • The portal answers before the phone rings.
  • One component library serves both registers.
